Goal: Determine the helium void fraction of a material (which is an IMPORTANT prerequisite for several other simulations).

Explaination (the numbers are taken from an instructive example):

The void fraction is the empty space of a structure divided by the total volume. In experiment it is measured
using helium, because helium does (almost) not adsorb. It would be consistent to also measure this fraction
using helium at room temperature. In practice it is easily computed from Widom particle insertion as the
void fraction corresponds to the new Rosenbluth weight.

The Rosenbluth weight, and therefore the helium void fraction in this example is approximately 0.80.
The pore volume is the void fraction times the unit cell volume. Note that the values dependent
slightly on the cutoff, and shifted vs. truncated potentials.
